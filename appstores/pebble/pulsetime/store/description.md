A clock you can feel, with guided practice and huge high-contrast digits.

A friend who is visually impaired asked me to build a way to feel the time.
That request became PulseTime. Press a button to feel the current time; during
lessons and time checks, one large black-and-white numeral follows each
vibration group.

This is a public beta. I would like to hear what works, what feels confusing,
and what you would change. Send feedback and ideas to
[luke@lukesteuber.com](mailto:luke@lukesteuber.com?subject=PulseTime%20Public%20Beta%20feedback).

## Your first time check

1. Install the app, then open **PulseTime** from your watch's app list. The first
   launch opens **Learn pattern**.
2. Press **Down** for the **3:32 hours** lesson. Down again reaches the minute
   phrase, then Down once more reaches the whole answer. **Select** plays the
   current example; press it again to stop. Replay as often as you like.
3. Keep **Terse Quarter / Standard** to start. Three hour pulses, a pause, and
   two quarter pulses mean **about 3:30**. The default answer covers 3:30–3:44;
   it does not give exact minutes. The visual clock still shows the current minute.
4. Down advances through the guide, Up goes back, and Back leaves it. Press
   Down on the final Ready page to finish. Hold Select from the clock to return
   to learning or choose Practice.

## Everyday controls

On the app's clock, **Select** tells the whole time, **Up** tells hours, and
**Down** tells minutes. Press the same reply button again to stop; another reply
button replaces the answer. Hold Select for learning and settings. Holding
Up or Down changes the haptic language.

To ask from your watchface, open the watch's **Settings > Quick Launch > Up**
and choose PulseTime. Repeat for Down if wanted. Hold a bound button from your
watchface to ask the time. Both shortcuts initially tell the whole time; the
app cannot assign those system shortcuts automatically.

Choose **Hold Select > Display > Bright / bold** for large black digits on white.
Dark layouts invert the colors. Exact-minute patterns and slower playback are
available when you want them. Haptic hours use 1–12 without an AM/PM signal.

The open clock also responds to one detected knock or flick. The optional
**Flick to tell time** phone setting uses two taps to launch the app from another
watchface; it starts off. There is no special touch spot: try two light knocks
on the same part of the bezel or case, about half a second apart. Wait for the
vibration to finish before retrying. Use Select inside lessons and menus.

## Help shape the next version

Tell me your watch model, pattern and speed, the time you expected, and what
you felt. Hard-to-read labels, confusing controls, missed taps, and rhythms
that blur together are all useful feedback.

I am also exploring vibration "fingerprints," or **haptic QRs**: learned signals
for information beyond time. A remote endpoint could someday turn a server's
healthy, degraded, or down status into a familiar rhythm. **Remote-endpoint
connections and automatic mappings are not implemented.** This release tells
the time. What else would you want to recognize by touch?

## Known beta limits

- The app's phone settings can retain older values after a missed watch update.
  Saving can restore those older choices, including the haptic language.
  Check every option before saving, then verify the language on the watch and
  replay a known lesson. Reopening the phone form alone does not guarantee a refresh.
- Terse Quarter's minutes-only :00 and :15 can feel alike. Use the whole-time
  answer if uncertain.
- Watch menus do not speak their labels, and Practice does not separately
  announce its target time. First-use assistance may be needed; independent
  blind onboarding remains unverified.
- The app has installed successfully on Time 2 and 2 SE. Pattern recognition
  still needs wearer feedback. Round layouts have emulator coverage; round
  hardware checks are pending. Store screenshots show emulators.

Supports Time / Time Steel, Time Round, 2 / 2 SE, Time 2, 2 Duo, and Round 2.
The original 2013 Pebble and Pebble Steel are not supported. The clock and
lessons work offline after installation. Optional pattern tones require Time 2
or 2 Duo; the other supported models use vibration.
